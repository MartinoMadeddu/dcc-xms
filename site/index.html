The idea

Everything stays live

Nothing is baked. A mesh is a cube node followed by the operations that shaped it. Change any value upstream and everything after it follows.

XMS | Imago is a 3D application built around one graph. Geometry, UVs and motion capture clips all travel down the same wires, and each node does one job to what comes in.

A mocap clip is a file node followed by the trims, renames and retimes applied to it. A model is a primitive, or a USD file, followed by its edits. Go back to any node, change a number, and the result updates.

The graph is cooked when its content changes, not on every frame. Moving nodes about, panning and orbiting the camera cook nothing.

Start from a template

Twenty-three templates ship with the program, one for each area of it. Each loads a working graph, laid out in rows and framed, and tells you what to try.

Never out of sight

Right-click an output and the node you pick lands under it, already wired. The ring at the left of a node bypasses it. The eye at the right shows it in the viewport.

Heavy data

USD, at production size

A file comes in as one packed primitive per mesh prim, shared and never copied. Pick the pieces you want; the nodes that follow work on those and pass the rest through.

The reader takes text .usda, binary .usdc and the root layer of a .usdz. Transforms follow the whole xformOpOrder: every op type, suffixed and inverted ops, stack resets. Z-up becomes Y-up, and the scene is scaled to metres when the layer states its unit.

Edit Poly on one wheel of the example car handles the ten thousand vertices of the wheel, not the 376,000 of the car. Without a pick, nodes merge everything and work on the whole.

Materials are drawn in the viewport: colour, roughness, metallic, opacity, the colour texture and the emissive texture of a UsdPreviewSurface. Textures decode in the background and each gets its chain of smaller copies.

The Load USD node lists what the file holds: materials with their texture files, cameras, skeletons, lights, and whatever is not read in full yet.

Modelling

A whole session in one node

Edit Poly keeps an ordered list of polygon operations in a single node, modelled on the Edit Poly modifier of 3ds Max.

Five selection levels: vertex, edge, border, polygon, element. Pick in the viewport, or select by rule, by normal or inside a box, so the selection adapts when the mesh coming in changes.

Move, rotate and scale manipulators sit on Q W E R. Each drag becomes a Transform operation you can edit afterwards. Give it a falloff and it is a soft selection.

When you are done, collapse operations one by one or all at once, or let the node collapse them as you go. Collapsed operations still replay when the mesh coming in changes.

The sea mine template is eleven operations on one cube, with a round of subdivision at the start and two at the end: about 9,600 polygons.

Texturing

UVs and UDIMs that think

Unwrap with least squares conformal maps, spread the pieces of a model over UDIM tiles by size and by neighbourhood, and look at a million edges without the editor blinking.

UV Unwrap cuts the mesh into charts where the surface bends past an angle, flattens each with least squares conformal maps (Lévy, Petitjean, Ray and Maillot, 2002), turns it to its smallest bounding box and packs the charts. Box and planar projection are there too.

Set a number of UDIM tiles and the separate pieces of the mesh are measured by surface, grouped nearest first, and handed tiles in order: the most surface on 1001, the smallest on the last. A palm and its fingers, modelled as separate pieces, land on one tile together.

UV Transform and UV Edit move, turn, scale and flip the whole layout or single islands. In the editor, click an island and drag it.

The editor draws into a pixel buffer of its own, on several threads, and redraws only when something changes. "Borders only" keeps the island outlines and drops the rest.

How the tiles are chosen

  • 1. Split the mesh into its unconnected pieces.
  • 2. Measure each by surface area.
  • 3. Merge the nearest groups first, with a penalty for a group that outgrows its share.
  • 4. Stop at one group per tile. Largest takes 1001.
  • 5. Pack each tile on its own.
Motion capture

From take to engine

Load a folder of FBX takes, split the characters, fix the pose, skin a proxy and write the files your engine wants, in one graph that runs over the whole folder.

A clip holds a skeleton, per-frame transforms, a rational frame rate and timecode, drop-frame included. The timeline has no range of its own: it takes range, rate and timecode from whichever node is selected.

Retarget puts the motion of one skeleton on another. Joints are matched by name, ignoring prefixes and namespaces, and bones are aligned in the rest pose, so the two may rest differently.

Nodes that choose joints by name take comma-separated regular expressions, with a picker listing the joints coming in and a live match count. thumb, index, middle works. So does ^Left.*(Arm|Hand)$.

Write FBX writes binary files with skeleton, animation, mesh, skin and bind pose, for one file or every file of the folder, in the background.

Workbench

An interface that gets out of the way

Every pane is a tab. Dock it, stack it, float it, lock it, save it as a file. Navigate the way your hands already know.

Eight panes: Viewport, Node Graph, UV Editor, Scene Explorer, Operator Stack, Properties, Primitive Inspector, Timeline. Drag a tab onto another pane, or to a side of the window to span that whole side. One padlock freezes the layout; dividers still resize.

The Primitive Inspector is a spreadsheet of the selected node: vertices, edges, polygons and face corners for a mesh, joints and bones for a clip. It is rebuilt when the graph changes and draws only the rows in view.

Layout, floating windows, theme, display mode and navigation style come back at the next start.

Type-in

Build it yourself

The whole program is open source. With Rust installed, three lines get you a build.

10 git clone https://github.com/srlegrand/xms-imago
20 cd xms-imago
30 cargo run --release
40 REM  Ubuntu or Debian, first:
50 sudo apt install build-essential pkg-config \
     libasound2-dev libudev-dev libx11-dev libxkbcommon-x11-0
